# Loudness

**Loudness** is the perceived intensity of a note — how soft or strong it sounds. Like [[pitch]], it's a *perceptual* quantity — your brain's read-out of the physical **amplitude** of the sound wave (how much the air pressure swings), related to sound intensity and commonly measured in **decibels (dB)**.

As with pitch and [[frequency]], the perception and its physical cause aren't the same, and the mapping is nonlinear — the decibel scale is logarithmic, because a very wide range of physical intensities is compressed into the loudness we actually hear. Loudness is also the quantity held fixed when isolating [[timbre]]: *same pitch, same loudness*, different instrument.

**Try it:** [[experiments/loudness_demo]] — run it with `pattern="ghost"`, then run it again with `pattern="accent"`. Same four snare hits, same pitch, same rhythm — only the loudness changes. That contrast, heard directly, is the concept: loudness is entirely separate from which note is playing.
