# Pitch

**Pitch** is the perceived highness or lowness of a note. It's a *perceptual* quantity — your brain's read-out of a sound's [[frequency]]. Everything else in this section refines it: [[frequency]] is its physical cause, [[timbre]] and [[loudness]] are the other qualities your ear reads off the same sound, and [[duration]] places the note in time.

Look at three C's spread across a keyboard — C3, C4, C5, each one an octave above the last:

![[music_theory/resources/images/pitch_keyboard.svg]]

Notice they get physically farther apart in *frequency* (each octave doubles it) while staying the exact same perceptual step apart in *pitch*. That's the core fact of this note: pitch is a perception, not a raw measurement, and it doesn't track frequency in a straight line — doubling the frequency doesn't sound "twice as high," it sounds like the *same note one octave higher*. See [[frequency]] for that (logarithmic) mapping.

**Try it:** [[music_theory/exercises/octave_up]] — enter the note one octave above C4 and get feedback that ties the name, the octave number, and the frequency together.
