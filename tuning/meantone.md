Meantone temperament came after [[pythagorean_tuning]] and before well temperament, and took a more uniform approach to the same problem: instead of leaving fifths pure and forcing the whole [[pythagorean_comma|comma]] onto one interval, shrink *every* fifth by the same small amount — a quarter of the comma is the historically standard choice (quarter-comma meantone) — so thirds built from four tempered fifths come out close to pure, sounding sweeter than the sharp Pythagorean third. The tradeoff: temper eleven fifths evenly around a closed twelve-tone circle and the leftover still has to go somewhere. It lands on the twelfth, uncorrected fifth, which comes out badly out of tune — the notorious "wolf fifth," left howling so the other eleven can sound clean. Meantone keyboards often physically dodged the problem by simply not building the wolf key at all, or by adding a split key to approximate two different pitches in its place.

See [[well_temperament]] for the next move: instead of concentrating the leftover onto one wolf interval, spread it out unevenly across several fifths so every key becomes at least playable.
